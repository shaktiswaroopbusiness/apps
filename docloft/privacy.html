Privacy Policy – Docloft

Last updated: 28 November 2026 (v1.0)

Docloft ("the app") scans and stores your documents (land papers, insurance, certificates, ID proofs and more) in folders on your phone, and resizes photos, signatures and PDFs to the size a form asks for. This policy explains what data the app handles.

In short: your documents never leave your phone. No account, no cloud, no upload. We cannot see your documents.

Documents and photos

  • Scanning uses Google's ML Kit document scanner, which runs on your device through Google Play services. The app only receives the pages you choose to keep. The app itself does not need the camera permission to scan.
  • Photo mode and form photos use the system camera or photo picker only when you tap a button. The app does not ask for permission to read your whole gallery.
  • Imported PDFs and images are copied into the app's private storage on your phone.
  • Text search: the app reads the text in your scans on your device (ML Kit text recognition) so you can search for words like a policy number. This text stays on your phone.
  • Passport photos: the background is removed on your device (ML Kit selfie segmentation). The photo is not uploaded.

Everything is stored in the app's private storage. Android backup is turned off for this app, so documents are not copied to any cloud. Uninstalling the app deletes them. You can make your own backup file (see below).

Sharing, saving and backups

When you tap Share, Save to phone, Print or Back up, the file is passed to the app or folder you choose. The app does not send it anywhere by itself. A backup is a single .zip file that you keep or send wherever you like; restoring reads that file from your phone.

Locked folders

A locked folder opens only after your phone's fingerprint, face or PIN check (Android's own screen lock). The app never sees your fingerprint or PIN. Locked folders are hidden from the Home screen and search inside the app; the files are stored in the app's private storage like other documents.

Reminders and widget

Expiry reminders are scheduled on your phone as local notifications. The home-screen widget shows your document count and the next expiry date, read from your phone.

Data we collect

We (the developer) do not collect, store or sell any personal information. The app has no account system and no server of its own.

Advertising

The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. To serve and measure ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK may collect and process your device's advertising ID, IP address, approximate location derived from IP, and app interaction and diagnostic data. This is handled by Google under the Google Privacy Policy. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services. Your documents, their text and your folders are never shared with the ads SDK. Where required by law (for example in the EEA and UK), the app asks for your consent before personalised ads are shown. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android Settings → Google → Ads.

Permissions

  • Camera (only for Photo mode, passport photos and form photos) — asked when you tap the button.
  • Notifications — for the expiry reminders you set.
  • Biometric — to open locked folders with your phone's screen lock.
  • Internet — only used by the ads SDK.

The app does not request access to your contacts, SMS, call log, location, microphone or all files on your phone.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13.

Changes
